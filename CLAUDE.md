## 1. What this is

A submission to the **DEV Weekend Challenge: Dog Days Edition**, competing for **Best Use of ElevenLabs**.

**Hard deadline: 2026-08-17 06:59 UTC.** Built, deployed, recorded, and written by then. No extension.

**Product.** Eight real shelter dogs. Each listing is read twice — once by a flat institutional voice, once by a voice *generated from that dog's own shelter record* via ElevenLabs Voice Design. One hero dog goes further: a slider scrubs through its stay, and the voice degrades as the days accumulate.

Two axes, one mechanism:
- **Across dogs** — different records produce different voices
- **Within one dog** — the same record over time produces a voice that gets tired

**Thesis.** Adoption is an emotional decision made from a bureaucratic artifact. Voice Design converts structured shelter data into an emotional signal. Waiting has a sound.

`intake_type: "unknown"` is the common case — most shelters do not publish why an animal arrived. It is a real value, not missing data, and it contributes no modifier.

## 6. The three things that decide whether we win

### 6.1 Timestamp-preserving audio swap

**Build first. Verify by ear before anything else.** Hearing the same sentence flip voices mid-word is the moment a judge remembers.

```js
function swapSource(newSrc) {
  const t = audio.currentTime;
  const wasPlaying = !audio.paused;
  audio.src = newSrc;
  audio.addEventListener('loadedmetadata', () => {
    audio.currentTime = t;
    if (wasPlaying) audio.play();
  }, { once: true });
}
```

**One shared `<audio>` element** for the whole page. State: `currentDogId`, `mode`, `heroDay`. Switching dogs stops playback.

The hero slider uses this same function — dragging swaps sources while preserving position, so the voice ages mid-sentence.

### 6.2 The attribute → prompt function

The intellectual core. **Real documented code** in `src/voicePrompt.js`, not a hardcoded lookup. The post shows this function.

```
BASE       age_band  puppy→"bright, very fast, unsteady, tumbling"
                     young→"quick, energetic, eager"
                     adult→"even, steady, measured"
                     senior→"low, slow, weary, patient"
PITCH      size      large→"lower, resonant"   small→"higher, clipped"
WEARINESS  days      1-29→(none)  30-119→"a little flat"
                     120-179→"quiet and tired"  180+→"flat, barely lifting, worn through"
GUARD      returns   1→"slightly hesitant"  2+→"anxious, over-eager, trying too hard"
STRAIN     intake    stray→"watchful"  owner_surrender→"resigned"  returned→"uncertain"
```

Signature: `buildVoicePrompt(attributes) → string`. Pure function, no side effects. Concatenate fired modifiers. Surface the derivation in the UI — showing the work is what scored for the previous Google AI winner.

### 6.3 The hero slider

**It is a demonstration of the mapping function, not a claim about a real dog's stay.** No shelter publishes intake dates, so no dog's actual timeline is knowable — the post states this outright. The slider holds one real record fixed and moves `days_in_shelter` alone, showing what the function does with that one variable. Label it as such in the UI; never imply the hero waited these lengths.

Four discrete stops, not continuous, labelled by elapsed time rather than raw day number:

| Stop | `days_in_shelter` | Weariness band |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 3 | (none) |
| Month 1 | 30 | a little flat |
| Month 4 | 120 | quiet and tired |
| Year 2 | 730 | flat, barely lifting, worn through |

Each stop lands in a **different** weariness band, so every drag is audible and every band the function can reach is demonstrated. Two tests enforce this — if the stops or bands change such that two stops collide, they fail. Stops live in `SLIDER_STOPS` in `src/voicePrompt.js`.

As the slider moves right the card should visibly cool — this is the one place motion earns its keep.

## 7. Visual direction

**Subject world:** a desk with a stack of municipal shelter records on it. The visitor picks up one record at a time and hears what it sounds like when that record speaks.

The card **is** an institutional document in flat mode and **becomes** something warm in designed mode. The visual transition carries the same argument as the audio. That is the signature element; spend all boldness there, keep everything else quiet.

**Reference:** `reference/waiting-room-reference.png.png` is the visual north star, supplied 2026-08-17. Treat it as art direction, never as content — its card copy is mock and contradicts the real data (it labels Yuji as Oregon Humane, "3 YR", and rewrites his listing). **Composition from the image, every word from `dogs.json`.**

```
--desk     #B9AB92   the warm surface the records sit on
--paper    #F2EBDD   the active record
--paper-2  #E7DCC8   the sheets behind it
--ink      #24201B   primary text
--rule     #B3A48D   document dividers
--warm     #D7A83E   ONLY the designed-voice state
--on-air   #C94732   ONLY the live audio indicator
```

Seven values, no eighth. Every tint, shadow and hairline is a `color-mix` of these — there are zero `rgba()`/`hsl()` literals in the stylesheet. The desk must stay clearly **darker and warmer** than the paper: that value gap is what makes the card read as an object on a surface rather than a panel on a page.

**Type — two languages, and the split is the argument.** The form is bureaucratic and the dog's voice is human:

- **IBM Plex Mono** for everything administrative — record number, shelter, age, breed, labels, controls, navigation, the derivation table. Small, spaced, quiet.
- **Source Serif 4** for anything the dog or the shelter *wrote* — the listing, the shared sentence in the masthead and the echo block, the project explanation. Set at ~19px/1.62 so it reads as prose, never as data.
- **Archivo Narrow** for the title and dog names only.

Never set a listing in mono: it turns a person's words into technical documentation, which is the opposite of the point.

**The structure is a card deck, not a list.** One record at a time on a desk, with the next few cards showing their top edges beneath it. Navigation by arrows, drag/swipe, arrow keys, and a tab index. Four parts: editorial introduction → deck → index → project explanation. The active card is the single visual centre; nothing else may compete with it.

**Physicality, kept sophisticated.** Subtle radius (5px), two-layer shadow, ~0.7rem offsets between stacked cards, one very quiet grain on the desk. No random rotations, no scrapbook, no big shadows. The card tilts fractionally while dragged and never otherwise.

**The two coloured states.** `--on-air` appears only as the ON AIR dot and word, only while audio is actually playing, plus the record counter and active tab marker. `--warm` marks the designed reading: the card's paper warms ~13% toward amber, and on Yuji that warmth *drains as the stay dial moves right* — the document cools as the wait lengthens. Never a yellow page.

**Type:** condensed grotesque for names and display (Archivo Narrow / Oswald); IBM Plex Mono for everything else because kennel cards are typewritten. Administrative labels are 0.6875rem uppercase at 0.18em tracking and always secondary. Listing text is never smaller than 17px.

**Quality floor:** responsive to 375px — recomposed, not shrunk — visible keyboard focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, visible audio transport.

## 11. The DEV post (Writing Quality is ~20% of the score)

Not documentation. An argument.

1. **Open with a real listing quoted flat.** Ask what it tells you about the animal. No preamble.
2. **The sentence four dogs share.** Then quote it again — and reveal it is Ponyboy's listing, and Hart's, and two others', word for word. Ponyboy is a 3-year-old Anatolian Shepherd, 77 lb; Hart is a 9-year-old pit bull who has been waiting far longer. The shelter's template cannot tell them apart. **This is the headline finding, and it belongs above the thesis** — it proves the argument from the source material instead of asserting it. The page detects these overlaps at load (`src/sharedText.js`, exact match, no fuzzy matching), so the count is checkable, not claimed.
3. **The thesis** — one paragraph. The listing text is 100% the shelter's; the only thing we add is the voice. The cheerfulness is the institution's, the weariness is the record's.
4. **The hero dog.** Week 1 → Year 2, same function, one variable moved. The emotional and technical centrepiece — and see item 8, which is what the slider is really for.
5. **The mapping table and the actual code.** Technical Execution evidence no other entry will have.
6. **Why Voice Design, not preset TTS.** Preset = picking from a menu. Generated = the record produces the voice. **Name the control honestly:** the flat reading is ElevenLabs' stock **"Roger"** — their own description is *"Charismatic, positive and conversational"* — on **Eleven Multilingual v2**, the same model as the designed voices. The control is not a monotone robot; it is the upbeat register shelter marketing is already written in, which makes the contrast one of **tone, not expressiveness**. That distinction is the honest version of the claim and pre-empts the obvious objection. (Free tier ended with 116 credits remaining.)
7. **Why pre-generated audio** — determinism, no key exposure, no rate limits. Engineering judgment.
8. **The variable we could not get.** **No shelter publishes an intake date.** Not one of the eight listings states how long the animal has been waiting, so `days_in_shelter` is `"unknown"` for every dog and — because we refuse to guess — **no dog carries a weariness modifier in its own voice.** Weariness exists on the page in exactly one place: the hero slider, which is explicitly a demonstration of what the function does with that variable, not a report of a real stay. The honest sentence is *we can hear what waiting does to a voice; we cannot tell you how long any of these dogs has actually waited.* The absence is the finding — the one number that would make this a measurement is the one number the sector does not publish. Yuji's listing says "way too long"; that is as precise as the record gets.
9. **Limitations, plainly.** The voices are an interpretation, not the dog. The mapping is authored, not learned. Eight dogs is not a study. The degradation curve is a design choice, not a measurement. These dogs may already be adopted.

Items 8 and 9 are counterintuitive and high-leverage. Most submissions overclaim; the one that knows what it doesn't know reads as mature.

Embed the demo video near the top. A missing video sinks otherwise-good entries.
